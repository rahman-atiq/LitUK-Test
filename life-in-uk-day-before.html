Two

The Traps

The confusable pairs the test uses to catch people, each with the memory hook that defends it. If you read nothing else, read this.

Early BritainStone Age – 1066
Memory hook "Caesar failed, Claudius nailed." 55 BC = failed visit. AD 43 = real conquest. And the three saints: Patrick → Ireland, Columba → Scotland (Iona), Augustine → south of England (Canterbury) — P·C·A, north to south.
Exam trap The Iron Age coins mark the start of British history — not the Romans. And Hadrian's Wall kept out the Picts (ancestors of the Scots), not the Vikings.
The Middle Ages1066 – 1485
Memory hook 1215 · 1314 · 1415 — the staircase of dates. Magna Carta 12-15, Bannockburn 13-14, Agincourt 14-15. Rising pairs, one century apart. Then the roses: red beat white, and Henry VII put white inside red.
Exam trap Bannockburn 1314 = Robert the Bruce beats the English (Scotland stays free). Agincourt 1415 = Henry V beats the French. Different century, opposite result for England. Also: the Hundred Years War lasted 116 years.
The Tudors & the Stuarts1485 – 1688
Memory hook "Divorced, beheaded, died; divorced, beheaded, survived." Three Catherines bracket the list: Aragon starts it, Howard and Parr end it. The two beheaded wives are cousins (Boleyn & Howard).
Exam trap Same man, two numbers: James VI of Scotland = James I of England; William III of England = William II of Scotland (William of Orange). And keep the pair straight: 1665 plague → 1666 fire — disease before flames.
A Global Power1689 – 1901
Memory hook Two unions, two "-oh-" dates: 1707 and 1801. 1707 joins Scotland → "Great Britain". 1801 joins Ireland → "United Kingdom". And Napoleon's decade: 1805 sea (Nelson · Trafalgar) → 1815 land (Wellington · Waterloo).
Exam trap 1807 = slave trade banned (British ships & ports). 1833 = slavery itself abolished (Emancipation Act, whole Empire). Also: 1918 = women over 30 vote; 1928 = women at 21, equal with men.
The 20th Century1901 – 1945
Memory hook WWI ends on elevens: 11am, 11th day, 11th month, 1918. And 1940 is Churchill's year of three: becomes PM → Dunkirk → Battle of Britain, in that order.
Exam trap TV dates: 1922 = BBC radio, 1932 = Baird's first TV broadcast (London–Glasgow), 1936 = BBC's first regular TV service. Three different milestones — the test loves swapping them.
Britain since 19451945 – today
Memory hook The 1940s trio: 1942 · 1944 · 1948. Beveridge reports (1942) → Butler educates (1944) → Bevan heals (1948). Three Bs, in alphabetical-ish order, each two years-ish apart.
Exam trap Bevan ≠ Beveridge ≠ Butler. Beveridge wrote the report, Butler passed the Education Act, Bevan built the NHS. And the EEC: founded 1957 without the UK — Britain joined in 1973.

The Ladder

Succession in order, from Bosworth on. The timeline can't show that Edward VI and Mary I were a religious about-face — this can. Years are accession years where the chapter gives one.

House of Tudor
Henry VII1485 Won Bosworth; married Elizabeth of York — white rose inside red
Henry VIII1509 Six wives; broke with Rome and made himself head of the Church of England
Edward VI1547 Strongly Protestant; the Book of Common Prayer; died at 15
↕Protestant, then Catholic, in one step. Two monarchs, two religions, six years apart — the chapter calls them the whiplash years, and the test knows it.
Mary I1553 Devout Catholic; persecuted Protestants — "Bloody Mary"; short reign
Elizabeth I1558 Re-established the Church of England; Armada 1588; died childless 1603
House of Stuart
James VI & I1603 Union of the crowns — Scotland stays a separate country; King James Bible
Charles Ito 1649 Divine Right of Kings; 11 years without Parliament; Civil War 1642; executed
The Commonwealth · 1649–1660 · no monarch
Oliver Cromwell1653 Lord Protector, not king; Dunbar and Worcester; brutal in Ireland; died 1658
Richard Cromwell1658 Succeeded his father and could not hold it together
House of Stuart, restored
Charles II1660 Invited back from the Netherlands; plague, fire, Habeas Corpus, Royal Society
James II1685 VII of Scotland; Catholic; favoured Catholics and ignored Parliament
William of Orange & Mary1688 Glorious Revolution — invited in, no fighting in England; Boyne 1690
Anneto 1714 Act of Union 1707 — the Kingdom of Great Britain; no surviving children
House of Hanover
George I1714 German Protestant, chosen by Parliament; poor English, so he leaned on ministers
The Georges in between1714–1837 The chapter names no monarch between George I and Victoria — nothing to learn here
Victoria1837 Queen at 18; reigned almost 64 years; the Empire at its largest
From here it is Prime Ministers, not monarchs The clock resets — Walpole (1721) overlaps the Hanoverians above. After Victoria the chapter stops naming monarchs and starts naming the people running the country.
