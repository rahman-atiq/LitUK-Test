## What makes this bank different

The other four banks are scrapes. Their source of truth is a website, they are
regenerated by a fetcher, and `validate-banks.mjs` checking the built file is the whole
story. Two consequences follow from this one being written instead:

**It has a second source of truth.** `tools/data/toughest/` is the authored copy;
`toughest-data.js` is a build artefact. Edit the JSON, rerun the builder. Editing
`toughest-data.js` directly is how the two silently diverge, and the next rebuild
throws the edit away. `tools/check-toughest-source.mjs` compares them on content and
fails if the build is stale.

**It can invent things, and a scrape cannot.** A scraped bank's errors are the
source's errors. An authored bank's errors are ours, and a wrong answer drilled on a
spaced-repetition schedule is a wrong fact rehearsed to confidence and then written
down in an exam. Everything in the next section exists to stop that.

The questions target what the scrapes under-drill: the discriminations the handbook
itself sets up to be confused. Confusable date pairs (1707/1801, 1807/1833,
1918/1928), near-miss names (Bevan/Beveridge/Butler, Holst/Elgar,
Chichester/Knox-Johnston), reverse-direction stems that ask for the country given the
date rather than the date given the country, and 82 two-option twists.

## The two corpora, and why the difference bites

There are two renderings of the same handbook, and they are **not interchangeable**:

| | What it is | Read it with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `life-in-uk-chapter{1..5}.html` (+ `-story`) | The repo's condensed rendering. **The corpus every check judges a bank against**, and what the app's "read why" links open. | `node tools/dump-chapters.mjs` |
| `britizen.uk/study-guide/` | The full handbook prose. Richer, and the current edition. | `node tools/fetch-study-guide.mjs` |

The guide says more than the chapters do. Write from the guide alone and you produce
claims this repo cannot support — **the first draft did exactly that fourteen times.**
Allotments, Morecambe and Wise, the soap operas, darts, "an association of countries":
all genuinely in the handbook, none of them on a chapter page. Each was re-anchored on
wording the chapters carry, which is why the unsupported count came back to 99.

The rule: **write against the intersection.** The guide tells you what the handbook
says; the chapters tell you what we can verify. `tools/fetch-study-guide.mjs --diff`
prints one measure of the gap — 55 figures the guide prints that no chapter page does,
including 2020, 2022 and 2024.

### Facts the two corpora disagree about — do not ask these

Not a style preference. These are live contradictions between the edition the repo
carries and the edition the site serves, and three of them are pinned as open findings
in `tools/test-verify-bank.mjs`. A question picking a side teaches one edition's answer
as if it were the other's.

| Fact | Repo chapters | Live study guide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Commonwealth member states | 54 | 56 |
| Northern Ireland Assembly seats | 108 MLAs | 90 members |
| Jury service age range | 18–70 | 18–75 |
| Current monarch | Elizabeth II, heir Charles | Charles III, heir William |
| National Lottery minimum age | 16 | 18 (from April 2021) |
| Brexit | not mentioned | 23:00 GMT, 31 January 2020 |

The bank asks none of them. If you want them covered, **reconcile the two sources
first** — that is a separate piece of work, and it changes `test-verify-bank.mjs`.

Facts where the two agree, and which are therefore safe and well used: jury 12 / 15,
small claims £10,000 / £5,000, 60 AMs, 129 MSPs, EU 27, Council of Europe 47, UN
Security Council 15 with 5 permanent, voting age 18 set 1969, National Trust 1895 by
three volunteers, 15 national parks, TV licence fine up to £1,000, the population curve
and the 84/8/5/<3 and 70/21/4/2/1 percentages.

## Adding tests 521 and beyond

1. **Read the ground truth.** `node tools/dump-chapters.mjs --ref --out /tmp/corpus`
   for the chapters; `node tools/fetch-study-guide.mjs` if you also want the fuller
   prose. Use `--grep` to check what the chapters actually say before writing a stem.
2. **Check the figures.** `node tools/dump-chapters.mjs --numbers` is the list an
   answer may draw a number from. Anything else is rejected — see the rule below.
3. **Write `tools/data/toughest/t521.json`**, 24 questions, same shape:
   `{"t": stem, "e": explanation, "p": topic 0-4, "o": [[text, 0|1], …]}`.
   Topic index is load-bearing and lives in `tools/lib/banks.mjs`:
   `0` society · `1` history · `2` government · `3` values · `4` what-is-the-UK.
   Every question needs a real `e`: the Mistakes view and `facts.js` both read it.
4. **Extend the block** in `tools/validate-banks.mjs` — `toughest.n` is `[[501, 520]]`
   and must grow to include the new test number, or INV-3 fails.
5. **Build and check**, in this order:
   ```
   node tools/build-toughest-data.mjs
   node tools/build-search-index.mjs     # search index FIRST —
   node tools/build-facts.mjs            # build-facts reads search-index.js
   node tools/check-toughest-source.mjs
   npm test
   ```
6. **Move the counts together.** `validate-banks.mjs` names them all when they drift:
   `UNIQUE_EXPECT` in `tools/validate-banks.mjs`, `UNIQUE_EXPECT` in
   `life-in-uk-mock-tests.html`, and `TOTAL_TESTS` / `TOTAL_QUESTIONS` /
   `UNIQUE_QUESTIONS` / the `<span class="tag">N tests</span>` in `index.html`.
7. **Re-run `verify-bank`.** The counts must still read 0 / 7 / 99 with nothing on the
   new ids. Anything else means a question is asserting something the chapters do not.
8. **Bump `VERSION` in `sw.js`.** Any change to a data file strands offline users on
   the old copy otherwise. (Missed on the first pass here, along with adding
   `toughest-data.js` to the precache list. Both fixed.)

### Ids and test numbers never move

`toughest` owns ids **3198+** and test numbers **501–520**; `lituktestweb` was capped at
3197 to make room. Saved progress — spaced-repetition boxes, mistake history, per-test
scores — is keyed on both. A question may be corrected in place, but reordering the
JSON reassigns ids and silently reattaches a learner's history to different questions.
A retired question's id is left as a hole rather than reused. `check-toughest-source.mjs`
asserts ids run contiguously from 3198 in source order.

## The rules the checker enforces, and why each exists

`node tools/check-toughest-source.mjs` — in `npm test`, so it cannot be forgotten.

| Rule | The failure it prevents |
| --- | --- |
| An answer may only state a figure the chapters print | The way an authored bank starts teaching a number the exam marks wrong. Invisible once built: `verify-bank`'s contradiction signal only fires when the located passage happens to carry a rival figure of the same kind. |
| A "which TWO" stem must key exactly two; two keyed answers must say TWO | A TWO-stem keying one answer is unanswerable. |
| No two options may collapse under the dedup key | The engine would show a question with two identical-looking choices. |
| No duplicate within the bank, or against the 3,198 already shipped | The engine collapses duplicates onto one id; the learner drills one question and believes they covered two. Caught one real collision on the first pass — a Caxton question identical to `g2837`. |
| Every question needs an explanation | `facts.js` slices fact cards out of `e`. |
| The build must match the source | The expensive one. Edit a JSON, forget the builder, and every check in the repo passes against yesterday's questions with nothing looking wrong. |

All six were proven to fire against a deliberately broken copy before being trusted:

| Break | Message |
| --- | --- |
| source edited, builder not rerun | `1 question(s) in toughest-data.js do not match the source — rerun "node tools/build-toughest-data.mjs"` |
| a "which TWO" stem keyed to one answer | `t501.json q4 says TWO but keys 1 answer(s)` |
| answer changed to "AD 417" | `t501.json q6 stakes its answer on the figure 417, which appears nowhere in the chapter pages` |

## Gotchas that cost time here

- **`build-facts.mjs` reads `search-index.js`.** Search index first, facts second. The
  lituktestweb plan records the same trap; it is still easy to get backwards.
- **The source checker must skip its own bank.** Once `toughest-data.js` is in
  `BANK_FILES`, `loadBanks()` returns it, and a naive duplicate check reports all 480
  questions as duplicates of themselves. Hence the `g < 3198` filter.
- **`sw.js` is a second wiring point.** Adding a data file means adding it to the
  precache list *and* bumping `VERSION`. Nothing in `npm test` catches this.
- **Chromium lives at `/opt/pw-browsers/chromium-1194/chrome-linux/chrome`**, not the
  `chromium/` path the environment notes suggest. A real-browser smoke test of the
  Practice Tests page is worth doing: it caught nothing here, but it is the only check
  that proves the engine drives the new tests like any other bank.
