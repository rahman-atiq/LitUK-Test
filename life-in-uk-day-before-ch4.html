The Traps

All six of the chapter's confusable pairs, each with the memory hook that defends it, in chapter order. If you read nothing else, read this.

The UK TodayCities · money · people
Memory hook “84 – 8 – 5 – 3” and “8 → 20 → 40” Population share: chant 84-8-5-3 (England, Scotland, Wales, NI). Growth: 1801 = 8m, 1851 = 20m, 1901 = 40m — the population more than doubles twice in one century.
Exam trap Scotland's capital is Edinburgh — Glasgow is its biggest listed city, not its capital. Wales beats Northern Ireland on population (5% vs under 3%), and the £ note ladder stops at £50.
ReligionChurches & saints
Memory hook “David, Patrick, George… then a long wait for Andrew” Calendar order: 1 Mar → 17 Mar → 23 Apr → 30 Nov. Two saints in March, one in April, one alone in November. Only the Celtic-fringe pair with holidays — Patrick (NI) and Andrew (Scotland) — get the day off.
Exam trap The monarch is the head of the Church of England; the Archbishop of Canterbury is only its spiritual leader — don't swap them. And “Episcopal Church” is the Church of England's name in Scotland — the Church of Scotland itself is Presbyterian.
Customs & TraditionsThe festival calendar
Memory hook Autumn's 1-2-3 punch: 31/10 · 5/11 · 11/11 Halloween, Bonfire Night, Remembrance Day — three dates twelve days apart. And keep Vaisakhi as the only faith festival with a fixed date (14 April); Easter, Diwali, Hannukah and both Eids all move.
Exam trap Diwali is celebrated by Hindus AND Sikhs — not Hindus alone. Eid al-Fitr ends Ramadan; Eid ul Adha is about Ibrahim's sacrifice — the test loves swapping them. Mothering Sunday floats with Easter; Father's Day is fixed to a weekday pattern (3rd Sunday of June).
SportChampions & venues
Memory hook 2012 — the golden summer One year, five names: Wiggins (first British Tour de France), Farah (5k + 10k gold), Ennis (heptathlon gold), Murray (US Open), Simmonds (Paralympic pool). Add the venue — Stratford, East London — and Britain's third-place medal finish.
Exam trap Two different wheelchair racers won the London Marathon six times each: Tanni Grey-Thompson (also 16 Paralympic medals) and David Weir (six golds over two Games). Redgrave = 5 consecutive Games, Hoy = 6 golds — don't let the numbers migrate between names.
Arts & CultureMusic · art · books
Memory hook Prize triangle: Turner paints · Mercury sings · Booker writes Turner Prize = contemporary art (1984, Tate Britain, Hirst & Wright). Mercury Prize = best album (September, UK & Ireland). Man Booker = fiction (since 1968, McEwan/Mantel/Barnes). Three prizes, three art forms — never let the test cross the wires.
Exam trap Two famous German-born Britons: composer Handel (citizen 1727) and painter Lucian Freud. Holst wrote The Planets, Elgar wrote Land of Hope and Glory — swapped constantly. And the WWI poets are Owen and Sassoon; Ted Hughes and Betjeman are later.
Leisure & PlacesFood · film · landmarks
Memory hook The age ladder: 16 → 18 16: National Lottery, and wine/beer with a meal alongside an over-18. 18: buy alcohol yourself, enter betting shops and gambling clubs. Everything self-service is 18; everything supervised is 16.
Exam trap 15 national parks — in England, Wales and Scotland (the handbook doesn't count NI). Loch Lomond is the largest fresh water in mainland Britain; the Lake District is England's largest park. National Trust: 1895, three volunteers — but 61,000+ volunteers today.

The Calendar

One year, 1 January to 31 December. The rail holds everything with a date; the moving feasts sit in their own band underneath, because the chapter gives them a season, not a day. Saints' days are green — they come from the Religion section, not this one.

The moving feasts

No fixed date, so no place on the rail. The chapter tells you when they fall, not what day they land on — learn the rule, not a number.

Shrove Tuesday (Pancake Day)day before Lent
Pancakes are eaten the day before Lent begins.
Ash Wednesdayfirst day of Lent
Lent begins; churchgoers are marked with an ash cross on the forehead.
Lent40 days before Easter
The 40 days before Easter; people traditionally fast or give something up.
Mothering Sunday3 weeks before Easter
The Sunday three weeks before Easter — it floats with Easter, unlike Father's Day.
Good FridayMarch or April
Marks Christ's death. A public holiday.
Easter SundayMarch or April
Marks Christ rising. Easter eggs symbolise new life.
Easter MondayMarch or April
A public holiday.
DiwaliOct/Nov · 5 days
“Festival of Lights”, celebrated by Hindus and Sikhs; victory of good over evil and the gaining of knowledge. Famous celebrations in Leicester.
HannukahNov/Dec · 8 days
Jewish struggle for religious freedom; a candle lit each day on the menorah.
Eid al-Fitrend of Ramadan
Marks the end of Ramadan; Muslims thank Allah for the strength to fast.
Eid ul Adhamoves
Remembers Ibrahim's willingness to sacrifice his son; in Britain animal sacrifice must happen in a slaughterhouse.

The three secular bank holidays — early May, late May or early June, August — have no religious meaning. Northern Ireland adds the Battle of the Boyne in July. Everything else on the rail is celebrated but worked through.
