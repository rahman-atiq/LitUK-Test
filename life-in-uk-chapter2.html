Learn the equation. E + S + W = Great Britain; GB + NI = UK. Every trick question in this chapter is a rearrangement of that line.
Watch the word “Great”. “Great Britain” never includes Northern Ireland. The official country name needs both halves: “…Great Britain and Northern Ireland”.

Four Nations, One Union

The countries of the United Kingdom

The UK is made up of England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. The official name of the country is the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland. The rest of Ireland is an independent country.

England+Scotland+Wales=Great Britain

Great Britain+Northern Ireland=the United Kingdom

Official name: “United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland” — the name itself proves NI is not in Great Britain.

Memory hook Three make Great, four make United Three nations share the big island — that's Great Britain. Add the fourth, Northern Ireland, and you get the United Kingdom. Great = geography (one island). United = politics (the whole country).
Exam trap “The rest of Ireland is an independent country” — the Republic of Ireland is not in the UK, not a dependency, not a territory. Any answer that pulls the whole island of Ireland into the UK is wrong.
2

Say It Right

What each name does — and does not — include

The handbook is fussy about names, and the test copies its fussiness. Learn what each label covers before you learn anything else.

“Great Britain”

  • Refers only to England, Scotland and Wales.
  • Never includes Northern Ireland.

“The United Kingdom”

  • England + Scotland + Wales + Northern Ireland.
  • Official name: United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland.

“Britain” / “British” in the handbook

  • The handbook uses “Britain”, “British Isles” or “British” to refer to everyone in the UK — all four nations.
  • So a “British” fact in the study guide applies to Northern Ireland too.
Memory hook Great = 3 · United = 4 If the question says “Great”, count to three (E, S, W). If it says “United”, count to four. If it just says “Britain” in this handbook, it means everybody.
Exam trap “Is Northern Ireland part of Great Britain?” — No. It is part of the UK. This single distinction is the most reliably asked fact in Chapter 2. Don't donate the mark.
3

Linked, Not Part of It

Crown dependencies · overseas territories · who governs

A ring of islands and territories is closely linked to the UK without being part of it. The test wants you to sort them into the right box — and to know where the actual governing happens.

Crown dependencies — close to home

  • The Channel Islands and the Isle of Man.
  • Closely linked with the UK but not part of it.
  • They have their own governments.

British overseas territories — far away

  • Several territories in other parts of the world — for example St Helena and the Falkland Islands.
  • Also linked to the UK but not part of it.

Who governs what

  • The UK is governed by the parliament sitting in Westminster.
  • Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland also have parliaments or assemblies of their own, with devolved powers in defined areas.
Memory hook Near islands, own governments · far islands, “territories” Crown dependencies sit next door (Channel Islands, Isle of Man) and run their own governments. Overseas territories are the far-flung dots on the map (St Helena, Falklands). Both are linked to — never part of — the UK.
Exam trap Don't swap the labels: the Isle of Man is a Crown dependency, not an overseas territory; the Falklands are an overseas territory, not a dependency. And devolved parliaments exist for Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland — England has no separate one; Westminster governs the UK.
